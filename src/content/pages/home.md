## Overview

Brazil is the largest country in South America and the fifth largest in the world by area. It shares a border with every South American country except Chile and Ecuador, and its long Atlantic coastline stretches for more than 7,000 kilometers. Inside its borders are the Amazon rainforest, the Pantanal wetlands, the dry Caatinga of the Northeast, and the highlands of the Brazilian Shield ([Ball et al., n.d.](/ps120/references/#ref-ball)).

## History

Portugal claimed the land in 1500, and for more than three centuries Brazil was a Portuguese colony built on sugar, gold, and coffee, much of it produced with the forced labor of enslaved Africans. Brazil declared independence in 1822, abolished slavery in 1888, and became a republic in 1889. In 1960 the capital moved from Rio de Janeiro to Brasília, a planned city built in the interior to encourage settlement away from the coast ([Ball et al., n.d.](/ps120/references/#ref-ball)).

## Culture and Society

Brazilians speak Portuguese, which sets the country apart from its Spanish-speaking neighbors. The 2022 census counted about 203 million people ([Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística [IBGE], 2023](/ps120/references/#ref-instituto)), most of them living in cities near the coast. Brazilian culture blends Indigenous, African, and European roots, which can be seen in its food, its music such as samba and bossa nova, its religious life, and celebrations like Carnival.

## Economy

Brazil has one of the largest economies in the Americas. It is a leading exporter of agricultural products such as soybeans, coffee, sugar, and beef, and of minerals such as iron ore ([Ball et al., n.d.](/ps120/references/#ref-ball)). Many of these industries depend directly on the land, soils, rivers, and climate that this website will explore.
